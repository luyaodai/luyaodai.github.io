About

In the Larman Lab at Johns Hopkins, I am developing a multiplexed, high-throughput platform that profiles antibody responses: which proteins a person's antibodies recognize, measured at scale using cell-surface expressed protein libraries and single-cell sequencing. Tools like these can help reveal what drives autoimmune disease and how people respond to infection and vaccines.

Day to day, that means designing experiments, building analysis pipelines in R, and judging whether noisy biological data actually supports a conclusion. Alongside the PhD, I am completing an MHS in Biostatistics at the Bloomberg School of Public Health.

I am most interested in where science meets capital: biotech equity research, venture investing, and commercialization. Before graduate school I worked on biologics manufacturing at Regeneron and GSK, so I think about a drug's path end to end, from the bench to scale-up to the clinic.

03

Health economics projects

Early value assessment · Oct 2026 – present · R

What would a lupus diagnostic panel need to be worth paying for?

A diagnostics company asks what sensitivity, specificity, and price a multiplex autoantibody panel for SLE would need to be cost-effective and affordable for a US commercial health plan. The panel triages rheumatology referral after a positive ANA in primary care. Planned approach: a decision tree and Markov cost-utility model, with costs from MEPS 2018–2022 and 2024, headroom pricing, and a 3-year budget impact.

Phase 1 of 5 complete · scope

The panel is hypothetical. Public data and published literature only.
